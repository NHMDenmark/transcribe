# Transcribing: a guide for contributors

You look at the image of a specimen label and type what is written on it. Other people do the same, independently. When enough of you wrote the same thing, the value is accepted. This page explains how to do it well, and what happens to what you type.

## Getting started

1. **Get access.** Your platform's administrators either let you sign in with Google or GitHub, or create an account for you with your e-mail address. In the second case open the sign-in page, choose **Forgot your password?**, and set your password from the link you receive by e-mail.
2. Open **Projects** and choose one. Read its description: it says what the specimens are and may link to guidelines for special cases.
3. Press **Participate in the project**. The platform shows you a specimen that still needs you.

## The form

On the left (or above) is the image: scroll to zoom, drag to move. If a specimen has several images, small thumbnails on the side switch between them.

You can see **all the fields** of a specimen on one screen, or **one field at a time**; the switch is on the transcription screen and is remembered for you. Use whichever is more comfortable.

For each field:

- **Type what is on the label**, not what you think it should have said. Keep the spelling, even a spelling that looks wrong. If the label has a typo, so should your answer, unless the project's guidelines say otherwise.
- **Suggestions** appear while you type. Choosing a suggestion helps everyone write the same thing, which is what makes values agree. Do not choose one that is not what the label says.
- **Several values** (for example several collectors): press **add line** for each one, in the order of the label.
- **Have doubts**: tick it when you are not sure of what you typed. It is stored with your answer, so that whoever reviews it knows to look more carefully.
- **Deducted**: tick it when the value is not written on the label but you worked it out (for instance a country from the place name).
- **Indecipherable** and **No information**: use them when the field really cannot be read, or the label has nothing for it. They are valid answers. Do not leave a field empty because you are unsure, and do not invent.
- Some fields ask for a **choice**: pick the one that fits. The list is the same for everybody on purpose.
- A field you find too hard can be **hidden** for yourself (a link under the field). You can restore it from the row of hidden fields. Some fields only appear when you reach a certain level.

Press **Submit** to save and move on, or **Skip** to move on without answering.

## Comments

If something is wrong with a specimen (a missing image, a label that belongs to another record), write a **comment** on it. Comments are read by the people who run the platform, and are listed on the **All comments** page.

## What happens to your answer

Each person's answer has a **weight** that depends on their level. For each field, the answers that are **identical** add their weight, and when it is enough, the value becomes valid. So:

- Your answer is never judged on its own; it is compared with other people's.
- Typing **exactly** what the label says is what makes answers match.
- Over time your validated contributions raise your **level**, and your answers weigh more.

## Your own page

**My submissions** lists everything you have submitted, and whether it was validated, rejected or is still waiting. **My stats** shows your progress and level. In **My profile** you can change your name, photo and language, choose whether your name is public, and whether you receive notifications.

You can **delete your account** from the profile. What you transcribed stays (it may already be part of the scientific record) but nothing in it identifies you any more: your name, e-mail and photo are removed.
